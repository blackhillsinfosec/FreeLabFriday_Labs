# Physical Access

Physical access attacks happen when an attacker gets into a place they should not be - a server room, an office, a data center, or anywhere they can touch hardware directly. Unlike most cyberattacks, this one does not happen over the internet. The attacker is physically there.

Once someone has hands on a machine or a room, most technical controls become useless. Full-disk encryption, firewalls, network monitoring - none of it matters much if someone can just unplug a drive and walk out.

---

## How Attackers Get In

Getting through a door is often easier than breaking through a firewall. Common ways attackers gain physical access:

- Tailgating - walking in behind someone who has legitimate access
- Impersonation - pretending to be IT staff, a delivery person, or a contractor
- Cloning RFID badges - copying someone's access card without them knowing
- Picking locks or using stolen keys
- Social engineering - convincing staff to let them in

The weakest link is almost always a person, not a lock.

---

## What Happens After They Get In

Once inside, the damage can happen fast:

- Plugging in a malicious USB device or implant
- Copying data directly off a machine
- Installing a hardware keylogger
- Accessing systems that are left unlocked or unattended
- Photographing sensitive documents or screens
- Planting network taps to intercept traffic later

Physical access often leaves no digital trace at all, which makes it especially dangerous.

---

## How These Incidents Are Detected

Most physical breaches go undetected for a long time. The ones that do get caught are usually found through:

- Physical Security Review - checking badge logs, door access records, and camera footage
- User Awareness Training - employees noticing and reporting something off
- Site Walkthrough - someone physically walking through and spotting things that should not be there

Prevention matters far more than detection here, because by the time you notice something, the attacker is usually already gone.
