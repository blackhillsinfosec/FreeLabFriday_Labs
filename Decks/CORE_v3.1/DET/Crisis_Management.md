# Crisis Management

When a cybersecurity incident hits, the technical side is only half the battle. The other half is knowing what to do, who does what, and how fast you can contain the damage. That is what crisis management is about.

Without a plan, even a minor incident can spiral. People panic, nobody knows who to call, systems stay down longer than they should, and data that could have been saved is lost. Crisis management exists to prevent exactly that.

---

## What Counts as a Crisis

Not every alert is a crisis, but some situations demand an immediate, coordinated response:

- Ransomware encrypting systems across the network
- A data breach exposing customer or employee records
- A critical service going down due to an attack
- An attacker with active access inside the environment

The common thread is that normal operations are disrupted and time matters.

---

## How the Response Works

When an incident is confirmed, defenders do not just start clicking around hoping to fix things. A structured response follows a rough sequence:

1. **Detect and confirm** – something is wrong, and the team verifies it is real
2. **Contain** – stop the spread before it gets worse
3. **Eradicate** – find the root cause and remove it
4. **Recover** – bring systems back online safely
5. **Review** – figure out what happened and how to prevent it next time

This process only works well if it was planned before the incident, not during it.

---

## Why Communication is Half the Job

During a real incident, the technical team needs to stay focused on containment. But at the same time, someone has to update management, notify affected users, coordinate with legal if data was exposed, and in some cases, contact law enforcement or regulators.

Bad communication during a crisis causes almost as much damage as the incident itself. It leads to wrong decisions being made by the wrong people with the wrong information.

---

## What Happens Without a Plan

Organizations that skip crisis planning usually find out the hard way:

- Recovery takes days or weeks instead of hours
- Sensitive data gets handled improperly during the chaos
- Evidence gets destroyed before it can be collected
- The same attack happens again because nobody documented what went wrong

A plan does not need to be perfect. It just needs to exist and be practiced.
