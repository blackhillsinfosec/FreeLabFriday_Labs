# Insider Threat

An **insider threat** happens when someone who already has legitimate access to an organization uses that access in a way they shouldn’t. This can be intentional (data theft, sabotage) or accidental (careless mistakes that create risk).

Unlike outside attackers, insiders already know the environment. They often understand systems, workflows, and security controls, which makes their actions harder to spot.

---

## How Insider Threats Usually Happen

Most insider incidents follow a simple pattern:

- A trusted user already has access to systems or data  
- They abuse permissions or bypass policy  
- Unusual activity starts to appear (data access, downloads, privilege misuse)  
- The behavior continues until someone notices or an alert triggers

Common causes include:

- Excessive or outdated permissions  
- Lack of monitoring on internal activity  
- Disgruntled employees or contractors  
- Credential sharing or account misuse  
- Poor separation of duties

---

## What Insiders Can Do

Because insiders already operate inside the environment, their actions can look normal at first. Examples include:

- Accessing sensitive data unrelated to their role  
- Exfiltrating files or customer information  
- Changing configurations or deleting data  
- Creating hidden access for later use  
- Misusing company devices to bypass controls

In many cases, insider activity blends in with normal operations, which makes detection challenging.

---

## How Insider Threats Are Detected

Detection focuses on behavior, context, and visibility rather than just signatures.

Common detection methods:

- **User and Entity Behavior Analytics (UEBA)** to identify unusual patterns  
- **Active Defense and Cyber Deception** to detect unauthorized exploration  
- **Endpoint Analysis** to observe local system activity  
- **SIEM Log Analysis** to correlate events across systems  
- **Permissions Audits** to find risky access before it is abused

Most investigations start with something small - an odd login time, strange file access, or unexpected privilege use.
